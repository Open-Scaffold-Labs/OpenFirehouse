# OpenFirehouse Trademark & Brand Policy

> **Draft for counsel review — not legal advice.** Trademark applications for
> "OpenFirehouse" and "Fire Hazmat" are in progress; this policy is written to
> apply whether the marks are registered (®) or claimed as common-law marks
> (™). Counsel should confirm scope once registrations issue.

## Why this policy exists

OpenFirehouse's **code** is free and open under the AGPL v3 — you can fork it,
modify it, and self-host it. The AGPL governs the *code*. It does **not** give
anyone the right to use our **name or logo**. This policy does.

The reason is protection for fire departments, not control over developers.
Without a trademark policy, anyone could ship a fork called "OpenFirehouse Pro,"
"Official OpenFirehouse Cloud," or "OpenFirehouse Enterprise" and a chief would
have no way to tell the real, supported project from a third party's
repackaging. The marks let departments trust what "OpenFirehouse" means. This
is the same separation Mozilla uses for Firefox and Red Hat uses for Red Hat
Enterprise Linux: open code, protected brand.

## The marks covered

- **OpenFirehouse**™ (word mark) and the OpenFirehouse logo
- **Fire Hazmat**™ (word mark) and the Fire Hazmat logo
- Confusingly similar variations and the associated product names

Owner: Open Scaffold Labs, LLC.

## What you may do — no permission needed

- **Use the software freely** under the AGPL: run it, fork it, modify it,
  self-host it, at any size, for free.
- **Say what your thing is, truthfully (nominative use).** You may state that
  your deployment "runs OpenFirehouse," that your product is "compatible with
  OpenFirehouse," or that your fork is "based on OpenFirehouse," as long as the
  statement is accurate and does not imply we produce, sponsor, or endorse it.
- **Refer to the project by name** in articles, talks, documentation, training,
  and comparisons.
- **Use the unaltered logo to link to the official project**, where it's clear
  you're pointing to us and not branding your own offering.

## What you may not do — without our written permission

- **Name a product, service, hosted offering, app, or company using our marks**
  — e.g. "OpenFirehouse Pro," "OpenFirehouse Cloud," "OpenFirehouse Enterprise,"
  "FireHazmat Plus," or any name a user could mistake for an official edition.
- **Imply endorsement, affiliation, certification, or "official" status** that
  doesn't exist.
- **Use or modify our logos** as the brand for your own product or service, or
  alter, recolor, or recombine them with other marks.
- **Register** our marks (or confusingly similar marks, names, social handles,
  or domains) anywhere, in any language.
- **Use the marks for a fork or modified version** in a way that could lead
  departments to believe it is the official, supported OpenFirehouse.

## Forks and modified versions — name them your own

If you distribute or host a modified version, give it your **own** distinct
name and branding. You may include a truthful, secondary "based on
OpenFirehouse" or "OpenFirehouse-compatible" notice, but the primary name and
logo must be yours. (This is exactly how the AGPL and the trademark policy work
together: the AGPL guarantees your right to fork the *code*; this policy keeps
the *name* meaningful.)

## Interaction with the other licenses

- The **AGPL v3** (`LICENSE`) grants code rights only — never trademark rights.
- A **Commercial/OEM license** (`COMMERCIAL-LICENSE.md`) does not by itself
  grant trademark rights; any right to use the marks in a commercial product is
  negotiated separately and stated expressly.
- The **Hosted Service** is the only offering entitled to present itself as the
  official OpenFirehouse service.

**dale@openscaffoldlabs.com**. We're glad to grant reasonable requests in
writing; we just need to say yes first so the marks stay trustworthy.
